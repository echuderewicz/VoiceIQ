What the browser will and won't let you do

Needs a gesture
Sound can't start until you click, tap, or press a key. That's why the engine reads "not started" until your first press — a page cannot make noise on its own.
Latency
The gap between your press and the sound. Typically 10–30 ms on desktop, worse on Bluetooth headphones (100 ms+). Fine for playback, marginal for playing an instrument in real time.
Scheduling
Audio runs on its own thread with its own clock. Anything rhythmic must be scheduled ahead of time against that clock — setInterval alone will drift and stutter whenever the page is busy.
Volume
No way to set the device's system volume, and on iOS the hardware volume buttons win. You only control your own mix.
Backgrounding
Switch tabs or lock the phone and the browser may suspend or throttle audio. Generated sound usually stops; a plain media element is more likely to survive.
Mobile quirks
iOS silences generated audio when the ringer switch is on silent, ignores custom output-device selection, and limits how many audio contexts can exist at once. Test on a real phone, not a simulator.
Polyphony
Hundreds of simultaneous voices are fine on a laptop; a low-end phone will crackle much sooner. Every voice you don't stop and disconnect keeps costing CPU.
Microphone
Input requires an explicit permission prompt and a secure (https) page. Pitch detection is possible but is your code's job — the browser gives you samples, not notes.
MIDI
Web MIDI reaches hardware keyboards and controllers, but it's a separate permission and Safari support has historically lagged. It carries note messages, never audio.
No plugins
There is no VST or AU support. Every effect is either a built-in node, a hand-written processor, or a WebAssembly build of someone's DSP code.
